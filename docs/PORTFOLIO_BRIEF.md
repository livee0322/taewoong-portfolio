# Portfolio brief

## Purpose and positioning

This portfolio helps a hiring manager, practitioner, or collaborator understand what TaeWoong Lee can take from ambiguity to a real output. His career started in design and expanded through content production, commerce, service planning, UI/UX, QA, and collaboration with developers.

The positioning is not “a designer who can do a little of everything.” It is a practitioner who learns the work a project needs and connects planning, making, and operation. The site should leave visitors with a practical impression: design is hands-on, and product, selling context, and implementation conversations are understood too.

## Audience

- Hiring managers need a quick, credible view of scope, contribution, and career progression.
- Practitioners need to see how decisions were made and how work moved across disciplines.
- Collaborators need to see a reliable working style rather than a list of software logos.

## Information architecture

| Area | Location | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home | `/` | orient visitors, show selected evidence, invite contact |
| Project detail | `/projects/[slug]` | describe one case study with a repeatable narrative |
| Selected works | `/works` | show compact visual evidence across formats |
| About, career, contact | Home anchors | keep early navigation concise until deeper content demands routes |

## Home sequence

1. Header — name and anchor/route navigation.
2. Hero — clear positioning before a skills inventory.
3. Identity — how the work expanded beyond design.
4. Selected projects — the strongest four case studies in an editorial sequence.
5. Capabilities — planning, design, content, and commerce in plain language.
6. Selected visual works — a curated evidence grid, not a full archive.
7. Career — progression from design to product.
8. Tools and workflow — work area first, tools second.
9. Contact and footer — a low-friction next step.

## Project detail sequence

Every detail page starts with a project overview, then uses available sections from the shared schema: context, problem, role, process, decisions, output, result/learning, gallery, and next project. LIVBEE additionally has placeholders for why, service structure, UX flow, UI, build, QA, iteration, and result.

## Content priorities

1. LIVBEE: service planning, UX/UI, QA, project management, AI-assisted development workflow.
2. New App Launch / GPA: planning, Figma, developer collaboration, implementation review, QA.
3. Product to Market: sourcing through promotion, live commerce, and sale context.
4. Shopping Live: product analysis, sales point, preparation, live operation.
5. Selected visual works: a carefully edited supporting record, not the primary narrative.
